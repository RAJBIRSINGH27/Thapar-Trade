% =========================================================
% 1. HIGHER-ORDER GOAL
% =========================================================

\section{Higher-order goal}

The project aims to provide a structured and reliable campus
marketplace for student-to-student exchange of used products at
Thapar Institute of Engineering and Technology. Students commonly
need to buy or sell textbooks, calculators, cycles, electronics,
furniture, hostel appliances and other academic or personal items.
These exchanges are currently facilitated largely through informal
channels such as WhatsApp groups, ION groups, personal contacts and
word of mouth.

% =========================================================
% 2. PROBLEM STATEMENT
% =========================================================

\section{Problem Statement}

Although informal channels enable communication, they do not provide
the functionality of a structured marketplace. Product information
becomes distributed across multiple conversations, making relevant
listings difficult to discover. Buyers cannot reliably search or
filter products by category, price, condition or availability.
Sellers may also fail to update or remove listings after an item has
been sold, resulting in unnecessary communication and inaccurate
availability information.

A further challenge is the reliability of marketplace listings.
For example, a student may jokingly or misleadingly list a product
belonging to another student as if it were their own, even though the
actual product is not for sale. A general marketplace does not provide
a campus-specific mechanism through which such listings can be
reported and reviewed.

Key bottlenecks include:

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Fragmented discovery:}
    Listings are distributed across multiple informal communication
    channels.

    \item \textbf{Weak searchability:}
    Buyers cannot consistently filter products by category, price,
    condition and availability.

    \item \textbf{Stale listings:}
    Sold products may remain visible because sellers do not update
    their posts.

    \item \textbf{Misleading listings:}
    Unauthorized, prank or false-ownership listings may be difficult
    to identify and resolve.

    \item \textbf{Lack of moderation workflow:}
    Existing channels do not provide a structured report-to-review
    process for disputed listings.

\end{itemize}

% =========================================================
% 3. PROPOSED SOLUTION
% =========================================================

\section{Proposed Solution}

\subsection{Overview}

Build a campus-specific web marketplace with authenticated student
accounts, structured product listings, search and filtering,
availability management, reporting and administrative moderation.
The platform will allow students to publish standardized listings
and buyers to discover products through a searchable marketplace.

The proposed solution consists of the following components:

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Student Marketplace:}
    Interface for browsing, searching, filtering and viewing product
    listings.

    \item \textbf{Authentication and User Management:}
    Associates marketplace activity with an authenticated student
    account.

    \item \textbf{Product Listing Module:}
    Captures title, category, description, price, condition and
    images, with validation before publication.

    \item \textbf{Listing Lifecycle Manager:}
    Maintains \textit{Available}, \textit{Reserved} and
    \textit{Sold} states.

    \item \textbf{Reporting Mechanism:}
    Allows users to report misleading, unauthorized, inappropriate
    or falsely claimed listings.

    \item \textbf{Administrative Moderation:}
    Provides a dashboard for reviewing reports and temporarily
    restricting, removing or restoring listings.

\end{itemize}

% =========================================================
% CORE WORKFLOW
% =========================================================

\subsection{Core workflow}

The principal marketplace workflow is:

\begin{enumerate}

    \item A student authenticates with the platform.

    \item The seller creates a listing by entering standardized
    product information.

    \item The system validates required fields before publication.

    \item The listing is published with an \textit{Available} status.

    \item Buyers search and filter the marketplace to locate relevant
    products.

    \item A buyer views product and seller information and contacts
    the seller.

    \item The listing can transition to \textit{Reserved} and
    subsequently \textit{Sold}.

\end{enumerate}

% =========================================================
% REPORTING
% =========================================================

\subsection{Reporting and moderation workflow}

The reporting mechanism is designed to address cases in which a
student lists another student's product without authorization,
including misleading or prank listings. A user can select
``Report Listing'' from the product page and choose a relevant
reason such as product not for sale, actual ownership, false
ownership claim, misleading information or other.

The report is stored in the database and becomes available to the
administrator through the moderation dashboard. The administrator
reviews the evidence and either restores the listing, removes it,
or temporarily restricts it where appropriate.

\section{Solution Approach}

This project focuses on building a reliable campus marketplace as
an integrated software-engineering system rather than introducing
complex research-driven algorithms. Development will proceed
incrementally, beginning with the database schema and authentication
layer, followed by listing creation and marketplace functionality.
Search, filtering, status management, reporting and moderation will
then be integrated with the core system.

% =========================================================
% ARCHITECTURE
% =========================================================

\subsection{System architecture}

The proposed system uses a three-layer architecture consisting of a
presentation layer, application layer and data layer. The separation
supports modular development and keeps user-interface concerns,
marketplace logic and persistent data management distinct.

listings, categories and reports. Final implementation technologies
will be selected according to team familiarity, development

% =========================================================
% FUNCTIONAL MODULES
% =========================================================

\subsection{Functional modules}

The system will be implemented through the following modules:

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Authentication and User Management:}
    The platform will associate marketplace activity with an
    authenticated student account.

    \item \textbf{Product Listing:}
    Sellers will provide standardized information including product
    title, category, description, price, condition and images.
    Required fields will be validated before publication.

    \item \textbf{Marketplace Search:}
    Students will search listings using keywords and refine results
    using category, price, condition and availability filters.

    \item \textbf{Listing Lifecycle:}
    Sellers will manage product availability using
    \textit{Available}, \textit{Reserved} and \textit{Sold} states.

    \item \textbf{Product Details and Seller Information:}
    Buyers will view product information, listing condition, price
    and relevant seller information before contacting the seller.

    \item \textbf{Reporting Mechanism:}
    Users will report listings they believe are misleading,
    unauthorized, inappropriate or falsely claim ownership.

    \item \textbf{Administrative Moderation:}
    Administrators will review submitted reports and may temporarily
    restrict, remove or restore listings based on the review.

\end{itemize}

% 5. EVALUATION CRITERION

\section{Evaluation Criterion}

Testing will be conducted at feature, integration and user-acceptance
levels. Individual functions such as authentication, listing
creation, search, status updates and reporting will first be tested
independently. Integrated workflows will then be tested to ensure
that data remains consistent between the user interface, backend and
database.

A small group of approximately 10--20 students will participate in
user-acceptance testing. Representative tasks will include:

\begin{itemize}

    \item Locating a product using search and filters.

    \item Creating and publishing a product listing.

    \item Updating a listing from Available to Sold.

    \item Reporting a suspicious or unauthorized listing.

    \item Reviewing a reported listing through the administrative
    interface.

\end{itemize}

Evaluation will consider task completion, errors, time required and
user feedback. The findings will be used to refine the interface and
workflow before the final demonstration.

% =========================================================
% 6. OPERATIONAL CONSTRAINTS
% =========================================================

\section{Operational Constraints}

The project will focus on a realistic semester-scale prototype. The
system will not claim to automatically verify physical ownership of
arbitrary products. Instead, it will provide authenticated student
identities, seller declarations, reporting and administrative
moderation.

Payment processing, delivery services, automated fraud detection,
AI-based ownership detection and complex recommendation systems are
outside the initial scope. Advanced features may be considered only
after the core marketplace is stable.

% =========================================================
% 7. SCALABILITY AND AVAILABILITY
% =========================================================

\section{Scalability and Availability}

The architecture is intended to support future growth without
changing the core marketplace workflow. Separating the presentation,
application and data responsibilities allows individual modules to be
improved independently. Free or academic-tier hosting and database
services will be preferred during development, while the design can
later be migrated to larger infrastructure if required.

The principal availability considerations are:

\begin{itemize}

    \item Keep listing and search operations lightweight so normal
    student traffic remains responsive.

    \item Store listing and report records in structured persistent
    storage.

    \item Keep moderation operations separated from the normal
    student browsing workflow.

    \item Use modular interfaces so deployment infrastructure can be
    replaced without redesigning the application logic.

\end{itemize}

% =========================================================
% 9. RISKS AND MITIGATIONS
% =========================================================

\section{Risks and Mitigations}

\begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Misleading listings:}
    Provide reporting reasons and an administrative review workflow
    so disputed listings can be investigated.

    \item \textbf{Stale availability:}
    Use explicit Available, Reserved and Sold states and allow
    sellers to update listing status.

    \item \textbf{Incomplete listings:}
    Validate required fields before publication.

    \item \textbf{Integration defects:}
    Perform feature-level tests followed by end-to-end integration
    and user-acceptance testing.

\section{Summary}

This project proposes Thapar Trade, a deployable campus marketplace
that converts informal student-to-student trading into a structured
software workflow. The system emphasizes authenticated users,
standardized listings, searchable products, explicit availability
states and a report-to-moderation mechanism for misleading or
unauthorized listings.

The project is intentionally scoped as a realistic semester-scale
prototype, prioritizing a complete and reliable core marketplace
before advanced functionality.
